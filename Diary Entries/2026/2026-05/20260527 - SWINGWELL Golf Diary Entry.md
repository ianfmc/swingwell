# SWINGWELL - Golf Diary Entry - 2026-05-27

SWINGWELL - 20260527 Golf Diary
Date:
May 27, 2026
Session Type:
Short Game Calibration
Duration:
45 Minutes
Focus Areas:
Lag Putting • Putting • Chipping • Pitching
Theme:
Control first. Precision second.
Objective
Today’s session focused on rebuilding feel, tempo, and predictability around the greens. Rather than chasing technical changes, the emphasis was:
clean contact,
consistent landing spots,
pace control,
and reducing variability under pressure.
The goal was to leave the session feeling composed and repeatable — especially from scoring range.
Session Structure
1. Warm-Up & Rhythm Calibration (5 min)
Began with:
short chips,
medium chips,
and 10-foot putts.
Focus was purely on:
rhythm,
centered strike,
and relaxed tempo.
No mechanical thoughts.
The intent was simply to wake up touch and establish athletic motion.
Observations
Contact improved when tempo slowed slightly.
Better results came from allowing the club to swing naturally rather than “placing” the ball.
2. Lag Putting (12 min)
Ladder Drill
Distances:
20 ft
30 ft
40 ft
Objective:
Finish inside a 3-foot circle.
Focus
Stroke length controlling distance
Quiet hands
Dying speed
Observations
Distance control improved noticeably once attention shifted from line to pace.
Longer putts became more predictable when tempo remained constant.
Around-the-World Speed Control
Multiple distances and slopes used to simulate on-course variability.
Key Insight
Good lag putting feels less like “hitting” and more like rolling the ball into space.
3. Makeable Putts (10 min)
Gate Drill
Worked on:
face control,
centered strike,
and start line.
Observations
Misses tended to come from slight face instability rather than stroke path.
Commitment improved outcomes more than mechanical correction.
Pressure Ladder
Required consecutive makes from:
4 feet,
then 6 feet.
Key Insight
Short putting becomes dramatically easier when speed control from earlier drills is solid.
Confidence builds upward from pace.
4. Chipping Session (10 min)
Landing Spot Matrix
Used multiple clubs to land balls on the same spot while observing rollout differences.
Focus
chest rotation,
quiet wrists,
brushing the turf,
predictable carry-to-roll ratios.
Observations
Strike quality improved when the body kept moving through impact.
Less manipulation produced cleaner contact and more predictable rollout.
Key Insight
The landing spot matters more than the hole.
5. Pitching Session (8 min)
Distances:
~20–40 yards
Used towel/landing zones to reinforce precision carry.
Focus
commitment,
tempo,
acceleration through impact.
Observations
Best shots came from committed motion rather than steering.
Deceleration remains the primary danger pattern.
Session Reflection
This was a strong “feel restoration” session.
The emphasis on:
tempo,
landing spots,
and pace control
created a calmer overall motion pattern throughout the short game.
The session reinforced an important principle:
Scoring golf is rarely spectacular.
It is controlled, predictable, and repeatable.
Key Takeaways
Pace control drives putting confidence.
Landing spots simplify short game decisions.
Quiet wrists + body rotation improve strike quality.
Commitment produces better outcomes than overcorrection.
Tempo remains the foundation of everything.
