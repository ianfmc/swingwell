4. Key findings
4.1 What is working
	•	Putting: The most significant performance improvement in the dataset. Putts per hole dropped from 2.06 (Jan 9) to a best of 1.67 (Mar 15) and is holding in the 1.89–1.94 range on full-length courses. Consistent structured practice — short and lag every session — is clearly translating.
	•	Penalties: Eliminated entirely since March. Four penalty strokes on January 9 cost multiple shots; zero since. Conservative course management and improved tee-shot accuracy deserve equal credit.
	•	Fairway accuracy: Solid on 9-hole rounds (75–83%), with the January 9 back-nine (11%) an outlier driven by mental fatigue on a long 18-hole round. The Apr 11 57% figure on a 6,197-yard course is respectable.
	•	No-warmup performance: Two of the best rounds in the dataset (Apr 11 and Apr 16) were played with no range warmup — chip and putt only. This is a meaningful pattern worth investigating deliberately.

4.2 The primary limiting factor: GIR
Greens in regulation is the single largest gap between current performance and a 13.0 handicap. A 13-handicap golfer typically hits 35–40% GIR. Current performance:
	•	Best GIR: 44% (Mar 15, par-3 course — shorter approach distances)
	•	Full-length course GIR: 11–33%, averaging approximately 20%
	•	April trend: declining (17% Apr 11, 11% Apr 16)

Every GIR miss requires a scrambling save. At a 20% GIR rate, roughly 7 of 9 holes require a chip or pitch to save par — and even an excellent short game cannot consistently recover from that rate. The scoring ceiling is determined by approach play.

The practice diary shows a heavy weighting toward short game and putting, with full-swing approach practice underrepresented. The slapshot drill and driver work have improved ball-striking mechanics, but the critical distance band of 100–150 yards — where most approach shots occur — has received limited structured attention.

4.3 Practice-to-round transfer
The following connections are visible between practice sessions and round performance:

	•	Putting (strong transfer): Heavy putting practice in all three months directly correlates with the putting improvement trend. The clock-line drill (March) and start-line work (February) appear particularly effective.
	•	Bunker play (partial transfer): Bunker work improved (4/4 out in Feb 12 session; 7/30 holed in March), but only 3 sand shots appeared across all rounds, limiting the data.
	•	Driver/full swing (partial transfer): The slapshot drill and rotation focus have improved fairway accuracy. However, the approach-shot distance band (100–150 yards) remains untested under structured practice conditions.
	•	Mental state (clear pattern): RED state sessions (Feb 28 — 15 topped shots) confirm that anxiety directly degrades mechanics. GREEN state sessions consistently produce better data. The warmup hypothesis fits this pattern.

4.4 The warmup hypothesis
A working hypothesis has emerged from the April rounds: chip-and-putt-only warmup may produce better scores than a full range session. Two data points:
	•	April 11 (Indian Springs, 18h): no warmup → parred hole 1 → scored 92, no penalties
	•	April 16 (Indian Wells Celebrity, 9h): no warmup → first ever birdie on hole 2 → scored 44 (+8)

A proposed explanation: range warmups can groove a specific swing thought that doesn't survive first-tee nerves, or can introduce mechanical anxiety. Arriving instinctively, without a recent "fix" fresh in mind, may allow a more natural rhythm. This deserves deliberate testing — three consecutive rounds with chip-and-putt-only prep, tracking scores and mental state.

5. Path to a 13.0 handicap by December
The handicap dropped from 19.9 to 19.4 on the strength of two April rounds. To reach 13.0 by December, a reduction of approximately 6.4 strokes is required across roughly 8 months. This is ambitious but achievable with focused practice.

The primary lever is GIR. If GIR improves from ~20% to ~38% while putting holds, scores on a 9-hole par-36 course would typically drop by 4–6 strokes. That single improvement accounts for the majority of the handicap target.

Secondary levers in priority order:
	•	Approach shot distance control (100–150 yards): The most underrepresented skill in the practice diary. This is where GIR is won or lost.
	•	Putting start-line consistency: Putts per hole is strong but the 9 o'clock left-break line (weakest make rate in March) suggests a specific gap in start-line control on breaking putts.
	•	Back-nine stamina (18-hole rounds): The January 9 back-nine collapse (11% fairways, mental fatigue) remains the one unresolved pattern. A 13 handicap requires 18-hole consistency.
