2. The 5 root causes
Every coaching conversation across 14 sessions identified the same five root causes of scoring volatility. These are not symptoms — they are the actual levers.

2.1 Tempo under pressure
The single most consistent finding across all 14 conversations. When rushed — due to time pressure, a group waiting behind, or perceived external pacing — your transition quickens, arms outrun the body, and you produce a steep chopping motion rather than rotational force. Low point becomes unpredictable and contact degrades sharply.
	•	Evidence: Feb 28 session — 15/30 topped shots under time compression (50% miss rate).
	•	Evidence: Front 9 vs Back 9 (Jan 9) — fairways dropped from 50% to 11% as fatigue built.
	•	Rule: A committed 70% swing always beats a rushed 90% swing.
	•	Cue: "Long through it" prevents chopping. "Brush forward" reinforces low point.

2.2 Left miss management
The hook/pull-hook is your only truly damaging miss. The push-draw is playable. The miss becomes more pronounced under pressure and fatigue, and is currently the primary source of penalty strokes.
	•	Evidence: Left miss appears in every month's data across both practice and rounds.
	•	Evidence: Jan 9 — 4 penalty strokes, all correlated with left misses.
	•	Strategy: Manage the miss, don't eliminate it. Aim right of left hazards. Play with 'fade feel' (face slightly more open through impact) to prevent over-closure without producing actual fades.
	•	Preflight cue: 'Backside out' + 2× exaggerated in-to-out rehearsal swings.
	•	Swing cue: 'Start it right, hold the face.' / 'Start it just right of the stick.'

2.3 Visual discipline
The most consistent in-session cue across all 14 conversations. Every significant contact breakdown — tops, pulls, fat shots — correlates strongly with loss of visual focus before impact. Conversely, maintaining eye contact with the ball through impact consistently produces solid strikes.
	•	Evidence: Jan 9 back nine — 'eyes drifting forward before impact; contact quality degraded.'
	•	Evidence: Mar 10 — tendency to look downrange occurring ~30% of the time.
	•	Cue: Watch the ball through impact. This is not a swing thought — it is a performance prerequisite.

2.4 Practice structure prevents over-analysis
Your performance is governed more by state and structure than by technical knowledge. When sessions are unstructured, you layer multiple swing thoughts simultaneously, which prevents stabilization of any single component and increases variance under cognitive load.
	•	Rule: One drill per session. One variable per block. One cue per swing.
	•	Rule: 'Drill → Transfer → Play → Score' is the correct session progression.
	•	Rule: Stats are telemetry, not control signals. Evaluate after, not during.

2.5 Outcome pressure removes your best golf
The 'last swing effect' — where you consistently produce your best shot when the session is already closed — demonstrates that your underlying mechanics are sound and accessible. The difference is psychological permission. When outcome pressure is removed, your system self-organizes effectively.
	•	Evidence: Two best recent rounds played with no range warmup. No outcome expectations → best scores.
	•	Evidence: 'When the session is already closed, urgency disappears and your system self-organizes.'
	•	Implication: The pre-shot routine and EP card exist to manufacture this state deliberately on every shot.

3. Complete drill library
Every named drill from all 14 conversations. One drill per session — choose based on the decision tree in Section 5.

3.1 Full swing drills
Slapshot drill
	•	Purpose: Body-driven motion, eliminating hand dominance, sequencing and low point control.
	•	Execution: Split hands on club. Rotate through impact leading with body, not arms. Cue: 'Rotate through,' 'Body pulls arms.'
	•	Volume: 2-3 sets of 6-8 balls. Set 1: exaggerated/slow. Set 2: normal tempo. Set 3: target-aware.
	•	When to use: Primary drill for sequencing issues or after time away from practice.

9-to-3 drill
	•	Purpose: Tempo, face control, low-point consistency, and swing arc structure.
	•	Execution: 9-iron at 50-70% speed. Swing from 9 o'clock to 3 o'clock position. Reinforce rotation, balance, and forward low point.
	•	Volume: 12-15 balls maximum per session. Do not revisit after the drill window.
	•	When to use: Strike issue — contact quality dropping or low point inconsistent.

Hands-to-slot then rotate
	•	Purpose: Sequencing correction — transition and rotational mechanics.
	•	Execution: Backswing → slot position → STOP → rotate through. Cues: 'Pause then turn,' 'Slot then fire.'
	•	Volume: 3 sets of 6-8 balls.
	•	When to use: Transition or sequencing issue — early release or hand-dominant strike.

Forward tee drill
	•	Purpose: Low-point training — ensures ball-first contact and prevents flipping.
	•	Execution: Place tee 3-4 inches ahead of ball on target line. Club must brush past the tee after impact.
	•	Volume: 5 balls maximum. Use sparingly to reinforce forward shaft lean.
	•	When to use: Hitting behind the ball or losing forward lean under pressure.

Start-line calibration (WhyGolf disc)
	•	Purpose: Face alignment and start direction — trains start line only, ignores curvature.
	•	Execution: Place WhyGolf disc 4-6 inches in front of ball on target line. Goal: 7/10 balls start within ±5 yards of intended line.
	•	Volume: 10 balls. Use at start of sessions when dispersion is drifting.
	•	When to use: Directional consistency issue or after a pull-dominant practice session.

3.2 Short game drills
SPCD — Short Putt Control Drill
	•	Purpose: Diagnostic baseline for start-line and stroke reliability.
	•	Execution: 25 putts each from 3 feet and 4 feet. Track makes. Use tee-gate alignment (two tees just wider than putter face on start line).
	•	Success benchmark: 85%+ (21/25) for two consecutive sessions before increasing distance.
	•	Promotion trigger: When 85%+ achieved consistently, move to 5 feet.

LPD-24 — Lag Proximity Drill
	•	Purpose: Distance control from 24 feet. Supports two-putt probability and eliminates 3-putt risk.
	•	Execution: 25 putts from 24 feet. Track three outcomes: inside 3 feet / holed / outside 3 feet. Include 9 o'clock left-break line (your weakest position).
	•	Success benchmark: 21/25 (84%) inside 3 feet or holed.
	•	Promotion trigger: When 88%+ consistently, extend to 30 feet.

UCD-20 — Up-and-Down Contact Drill
	•	Purpose: Pitching strike quality from 15-25 yards. Isolates contact as primary variable.
	•	Execution: 25 reps from 15-25 yards. Track solid contact vs thin/fat misses. Secondary metric: inside 15 feet.
	•	Success benchmark: 89%+ solid contact (22/25).
	•	Key cue: 7:30 backswing length for 15-yard chips. 'Brush forward' for low point.

Spin ladder drill (3-1-1-1)
	•	Purpose: Trajectory and rollout control on pitch shots.
	•	Execution: Hit 3 shots at normal trajectory, 1 higher, 1 lower, 1 normal. Observe rollout differences at identical carry distances.
	•	When to use: Stage 4 wedge work — when you can already control landing window and want to control post-landing behavior.

Bunker commitment drill
	•	Purpose: Commitment and trust in bunker shots — eliminates deceleration.
	•	Execution: Focus on 'loud splash.' Weight forward. Committed acceleration. Cue: 'Splash, not steer.'
	•	Success benchmark: Final 6 consecutive shots within 9 feet.

3.3 Pressure simulation
Pressure finish
	•	Purpose: Replicates real-game scoring situations under pressure.
	•	Execution: 5 putts from 5 feet at end of every session. Must hole 4 of 5 to finish. No extra balls. No second chances.
	•	Up-and-down challenge: 6 balls, each treated as full scoring opportunity (chip + one putt). Success benchmark: 3/6 conversions.

6. Pre-shot routine
Formalized from your current preflight routine plus the 8-second reset from coaching conversations. This is your 'minimum viable swing' — the one system that travels to the course.

Step
Action
1 — Behind ball
Stand behind ball. Read the shot. Pick ONE specific target. Select club. Decision is final.
2 — Lineup
Backside out. Align parallel to target line. No second-guessing.
3 — Rehearsal
2× exaggerated in-to-out rehearsal swings. Feel the path. Cue: 'Start it right, hold the face.'
4 — Address
Step into the ball. One look at the target. Eyes back to the ball.
5 — Trigger & swing
One slow breath. Execute. Watch the ball through impact. No early head movement.
On-course EP
If ANY EP trigger appears before step 5: step off completely, reset from step 1.

7. Emergency procedures

7.1 EP triggers — recognize immediately
	•	Time pressure or rushing
	•	Desire to 'end on a good one'
	•	Rising frustration / seeing red
	•	Repeating same miss 2+ times without resolution
	•	Accumulating swing thoughts (more than one active)
	•	Loss of tempo or balance
Recognition itself constitutes the emergency. Action follows automatically without debate.

7.2 Stop ritual
	•	Pre-declare stopping condition before every session (time or ball count). Do not alter mid-session.
	•	Second-to-last ball: explicitly non-evaluative. 'This one does not count.'
	•	Final ball: committed execution with no outcome dependence. 'I'm done either way.'
	•	No additional balls under any circumstances.

7.3 Walk-away anchor (physical reset)
	•	Deliberate club replacement in bag.
	•	One slow breath.
	•	Relax jaw and shoulders.
	•	Walk to next position before any evaluation.

7.4 Yellow-day protocol (YELLOW mental state)
	•	10-15 minutes maximum.
	•	One club, one target.
	•	Immediate stop upon urgency signal.
	•	Success defined as calm exit, not outcome quality.

7.5 On-course emergency — 8-second reset
	•	Step behind ball.
	•	One inhale, one exhale.
	•	One rehearsal swing at 70% effort.
	•	Execute. This prevents subconscious acceleration and maintains tempo without slowing pace of play.

8. Course management system
Player Mode rules — no analysis or swing thoughts during a round. You are executing, not experimenting.

8.1 Decision loop (every shot)
	•	Get yardage.
	•	Select club relative to stock yardage (9-iron = 120 yards ±4.5).
	•	Aim center of green / middle of fairway. Never at pins.
	•	Execute with stock tempo (70-80%).
	•	Accept result. No mid-round correction.

8.2 Miss management
	•	Good miss: Push-draw. Playable. Accept and move on.
	•	Bad miss: Hook/pull. If it happens: smart bogey is the target. Never compound.
	•	Tee shot rule: Aim right of left hazards. Middle of fairway always preferred over aggressive line.
	•	Approach rule: Middle of green. Pins are low-value targets relative to the risk of short-siding.

8.3 Fatigue management (18-hole rounds)
	•	Jan 9 data: fairways dropped from 50% (front) to 11% (back). Fatigue threshold appears around holes 10-13.
	•	At holes 10+: drop to 65-70% effort on all full swings. Do not chase distance.
	•	If visual discipline drops: invoke 8-second reset before every shot.
	•	Score does not get calculated until after hole 9. Stats are telemetry, not control signals.

8.4 Warmup strategy
Two consecutive best rounds were played with chip-and-putt warmup only. Working hypothesis: full range sessions can groove a swing thought that doesn't survive first-tee nerves.
	•	Test: 3 consecutive rounds with chip-and-putt only. Track scores and mental state.
	•	Pre-round putting protocol: 10 lag putts (calibration) + 6 short putts from 4-6 feet + 3 pressure putts (must hole 2/3).
