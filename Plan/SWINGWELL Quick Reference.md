The 5 things that matter most
1
Watch the ball through impact. Every. Single. Swing.
2
Tempo first. A committed 70% swing beats a rushed 90% swing always.
3
Manage the left miss — don't eliminate it. Aim right of hazards. Accept push-draw.
4
One thought per swing. One drill per session. One target per shot.
5
Your best golf happens when outcome pressure is removed. Trust the process.

Pre-shot routine (every shot, every time)
Step
Action
1 — Behind ball
Read the shot. Pick precise target. Decide club. Done.
2 — Lineup
Backside out. Set up square to target line.
3 — Rehearsal
2× exaggerated in-to-out rehearsal swings. Feel the path.
4 — Address
Step in. One look at target. Eyes back to ball.
5 — Trigger
One slow breath. Swing. Watch ball through impact.

Emergency procedure (EP) — invoke immediately when:
Trigger
Response
Rushing or time pressure
Step off. One breath. Restart routine.
Want to end on a good one
That's the EP trigger. Stop now.
Rising frustration
Walk away anchor: re-rack club, slow breath, relax jaw.
Repeating same miss 2× in a row
Change club or change drill. Never chase.
Swing thoughts multiplying
One cue only: 'Start it right, hold the face.'
Loss of tempo or balance
Drop to 70% effort. Finish balanced.

Course management (Player Mode — no analysis during round)
Decision
Rule
Tee shot
Middle of fairway. Never at pins or tight lines. 70-80% effort.
Club selection
Stock yardage: 9-iron = 120 yards (±4.5 yd). Build all decisions from this.
Miss bias
Aim right of left hazards. Push-draw is the good miss. Hook is the bad miss.
Recovery
Smart bogey is the target. Never compound a mistake.
Putting
Lag: inside 3 feet. Short: start line over pace. No 3-putts.
Score
Do not calculate until after 9 holes. Stats are telemetry, not control signals.
